7c · Follow-up experiments (1 November)

Seeds 43 and 44 of the ablation, poisoning sweep and zero-day; an adaptive attacker; weight pruning; the clip comparison; a time-ordered split; and a breakdown of the 20-client run. Every table is generated from the result files by tools/build_followups.py. Details and caveats are in PAPER_NOTES §8.14.

  • Across three seeds the conclusions hold: attack recall 0.995–1.000 up to 50% attackers, plain FedAvg and trimmed-mean-only wreck the autoencoder at 30%, the filter fails at 50%, cascade zero-day detection averages 0.943 / 0.952 / 0.952. Autoencoder-alone recall and attacker survival move with the seed (ranges, not points).
  • Adaptive attacker: the filter mostly misses a white-box stealth attacker (survival 0.72–0.90); damage is bounded (known-attack recall ≥ 0.9997) but autoencoder-alone recall falls to 0.499 at 40% attackers. Zero-day detection under it is not measured.
  • Pruning: pruned during training, 70% of the autoencoder's weights can go with autoencoder recall 0.744 → 0.688; pruned after training it collapses (0.742 → 0.273 at 50%). Sizes are computed, not transmitted; updates are still dense.
  • Time-ordered split: accuracy 0.994, macro F1 0.913, but mostly because MITM (one connection) lands wholly in the test side; without it about 0.978.
  • 20-client dip explained: one missed Fingerprinting row out of 4.
